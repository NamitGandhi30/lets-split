My recommendation: blend them, with one look throughout

Which direction goes where
  • Shell and home from Midnight Sheet: a dark balance header, trip cards stacked per trip, a white sheet for expenses and a floating tab bar.
  • Splitting from Ticket & Ring: the ring with avatar pins, receipts as tickets, the “Requested” stamp and the tear-off on settle-up.
  • Uneven splits use the pill sliders. Drag one person's pill and everyone else rebalances.
  • Stats and Trip Wrapped from Bloom: the petal chart as the hero of the trip summary.
Shared across every screen
  • One type system: Bricolage Grotesque for big amounts, Geist for interface text, Geist Mono for decimals and receipt lines. Amounts always show smaller decimals, like ₹3,600.00.
  • Two springs: a snappy one for the interface and a bouncy one for celebrations. Every animation uses one of them, so the app feels like one system.
  • Color per trip: each trip has an accent (Goa yellow, Manali teal, Jaipur blue) that colors its card, ring and charts.
  • Respects Reduce Motion on iPhone.

Notes from the trend research

I avoided the lavender everywhere

Your references 1 and 2 lean soft purple. Purple-on-white is the most common look of AI-generated apps right now, so I kept the cool, airy tint and used cobalt and sunshine yellow as accents.

The dark mode isn't one red accent on black

That pairing is another overused default. Reference 4's stack of colored cards gives each trip its own identity instead.

Sheets and bottom controls are the 2026 norm

Primary actions sit near the thumb, and secondary content lives in sheets. Blur is used only where one layer sits over another, with a solid fallback.

Motion should mean something, and be easy to stop

Celebrate settle-ups and show cause and effect when splitting. Keep lists and forms calm, and honor iPhone's Reduce Motion setting.

Fonts with personality

Designers are moving away from Inter and Helvetica. Expressive faces like Bricolage Grotesque are used for display, with clean UI faces for running text. Every pick here is a free Google Font.

An iPhone “Liquid Glass” look is only approximate on the web

There is no official CSS for it. Background blur plus saturation gets close, so I'd use it sparingly on the tab bar and sheets.
